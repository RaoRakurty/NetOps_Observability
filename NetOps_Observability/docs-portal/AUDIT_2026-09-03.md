## 3. Style audit

### 3.1 What the benchmark vendors do

Measured from primary sources: the FortiOS 7.6.0, FortiManager and
FortiAnalyzer administration guides (1,324 + 973 + 615 topics, ~150 pages of
prose fetched); the PAN-OS 10.2 and 11.1 Administrator's Guides (1,198 and
1,484 pages, extracted to text so voice could be measured) plus the 11.1 release
notes; and CrowdStrike's fully public Falcon LogScale library (6,885 pages) and
`developer.crowdstrike.com` (444 pages).

**Measured facts:**

- Palo Alto: `you` appears 4,826 times, `we` 35 times. Median sentence 17 words.
- Fortinet: median sentence **12 words**.
- `powerful` and `best-in-class` appear **zero times** in all three corpora.
- Palo Alto's `STEP N |` step marker: 1,427 occurrences. They author in DITA.
- Fortinet's `To configure X:` procedure lead-in: 285 instances; 96.8 % start
  with "To", 98.2 % end in a colon. `…in the GUI:` and `…in the CLI:` are paired
  siblings.
- CrowdStrike defines seven admonition types and uses three: `note` 74,
  `important` 36, `warning` 2, across 340 sampled pages. `Tip` and `Caution`
  produced zero hits.

**Conventions worth adopting, in order of value to this product:**

1. **A permissions block above every procedure.** CrowdStrike boxes "Security
   Requirements and Controls" naming the exact permission a task needs, repeated
   at each sub-procedure. For a platform whose build rules make tenant isolation
   mandatory on every feature, this is the documentation counterpart.
2. **`shortdesc` grammar keyed to page type** (Palo Alto): a task's abstract is
   an imperative verb plus the benefit; a concept's says what the thing is; a
   reference's is a bare noun phrase.
3. **A published sample-data rule** (Fortinet's RFC 1918/5737/3849 policy,
   CrowdStrike's `example.com` and "John Doe" policy). Turns "no real addresses
   in docs" from a review comment into a rule.
4. **`To do X:` as a procedure lead-in** rather than another heading level.
5. **A release-notes contract**: a closed set of entry types, a class taxonomy,
   ISO dates, and an upgrade summary table whose column tooltips name the
   failure mode (`Downgrades To`: "downgrading beyond this version may corrupt
   or disable your deployment").
6. **A troubleshooting article skeleton**: Condition or Error → Causes →
   Solutions.
7. **Long descriptive alt text** on every screenshot.
8. **One estate.** CrowdStrike runs three doc properties with three generators,
   three glossaries and no shared search; the parts are excellent and the whole
   is not navigable.

**Anti-patterns observed at the benchmarks, to lint against:**

- Title case not enforced. Title Case and sentence case coexist inside the same
  CrowdStrike book, and inside its own glossary.
- Stacked competing openers: a machine-written abstract ("The documentation
  explains how to…") on top of a human first paragraph.
- Marketing pages mixed into product documentation (`Cybersecurity 101`).
- Flagship release notes behind a login, with only a marketing-shaped quarterly
  highlights page in public.

### 3.2 What is wrong with the Correlix portal's voice

Measured by `docs-portal/tests/voice.test.js` against the 71 pages as they
stood:

| Check | Failures |
|---|--:|
| Em-dash discipline (one per paragraph, ~one per 250 words) | **419** |
| Front matter (title, description, `page_type`) | 75 |
| Sentences over 45 words | 52 |
| Emoji in a page body | 34 |
| First-person plural ("we", "our", "let's") | 24 |
| Banned words and marketing filler | 10 |
| Dead internal links | 1 |

The em-dash count is the headline. 419 violations across 76,000 words is a
stream of asides where a vendor guide would use a full stop, and it is the
single clearest signal that the prose was machine-written.

Secondary tells found by hand: the landing page is a marketing hero with a
gradient headline and six emoji cards; `noc-guide/overview` frames the product
as a story; `simply`, `easily`, `just`, `journey` and `unlock` appear in
procedures; and the theme itself is glassy and glowing rather than dense and
printable.

### 3.3 The rules adopted

Written up in full in [`docs-portal/STYLE.md`](STYLE.md), enforced by
[`docs-portal/tests/voice.test.js`](tests/voice.test.js). In summary:

- Second person, present tense, imperative for steps. Never "we".
- Sentences under 45 words, most under 25.
- A denylist of 60 marketing and machine-prose phrases, plus a ban on the
  meta-voice opener ("This page explains…").
- An em-dash budget: one per paragraph, roughly one per 250 words.
- No emoji.
- Five page types (`task`, `concept`, `reference`, `index`, `release`), each
  with a required shape. Task pages must carry Before you begin → Steps →
  Result (or What you see) → Related, in that order, and must be titled and
  abstracted with an imperative verb.
- Honesty is part of the voice: absent is documented as absent, never as zero;
  "not connected" and "empty" are never collapsed; an empty list means not
  evaluated, not clean.
- A fixed terminology table: Correlix, Iris, device, interface, RCA case,
  incident, alert, finding, seam, tenant, organization, operator, collector, the
  console. Never "the NOC" as the owner of a fault.
- A sample-data rule: documentation-reserved addresses, `example.com`, Acme,
  private AS numbers, and lab captures.

### 3.4 The information architecture adopted

Nine sections, ordered by the reader's journey rather than by the code's
modules:

1. **Get started** — what it is, what the words mean, and a 15-minute path to a
   monitored device.
2. **Deploy** — requirements, sizing, reference capacity, install, air-gapped
   install, TLS, optional modules, verification, upgrade, backup and restore.
3. **Operate** — operator guide, onboard devices, send data, monitoring and
   alerting, incidents, incident response, infrastructure, explore, automation,
   dashboards and reports.
4. **Investigate** — RCA, the investigation workspace, protocol diagnostics,
   IGP health, interfaces by routing instance, and Iris.
5. **Security** — CTEM: findings, exposures, exposure stories, compliance,
   detection rules, configuration backup and drift, packet capture, transport
   security.
6. **BGP operations** — watchlist, prefix investigation, RPKI, AS paths,
   geofeed, alerting, bogons, BMP.
7. **Administration** — identity, tenants and organizations, authentication,
   API access, audit, data-collection administration.
8. **Reference** — API, feature flags, alert rules, connectivity, metric
   families, glossary, honest states, troubleshooting.
9. **Release notes** — what's new, by month.

The sidebar is declared explicitly in `sidebars.js` rather than generated from
the folder tree, so a page's place in the navigation is independent of its file
path. That is what lets the ten slugs pinned by `docs_index_test.go` stay where
they are while the reader sees a coherent structure.

## 6. Things this audit could not verify

- **`/api/security/frameworks` and `/api/security/compliance` are in the working
  tree but not in `HEAD`.** They are registered in `src/backend/main.go` and
  listed in `openapi.go` in the working copy, but a diff against `HEAD` shows
  all three route entries are uncommitted, and the running lab stack answers
  `404` for both. The generated API reference is built from the working tree, so
  it includes them. Treat those two routes as unreleased until the change lands.
- **`POST /api/troubleshoot/protocol-diagnostics/export` is advertised but not
  routed.** The handler exists (`src/backend/protocol_diagnostics.go`) and
  `openapi.go` lists the operation, but `src/backend/main.go` registers only
  `catalog`, `analyze` and `collect` (lines 2072–2074). The route therefore does
  not exist on a running deployment. The generated API reference advertises it
  because it is generated from the route table. **This is a product defect the
  documentation work found, not a documentation defect**: either register the
  handler or drop the entry from `apiRoutes`. Until it is settled, the
  "Export a redacted bundle for vendor support" page documents the console
  control, which builds the bundle from the analysis response, and does not
  print the route.
- **Recording an RCA verdict is gated on `alerts:write`,** not
  `infrastructure:write` as the route summary implies. Worth reconciling in
  `openapi.go`.
- **The comment in `internal/alertwebhook/hostroute.go` says "the four page
  conditions".** There are nine rules labelled `tier: page` in
  `src/config/rules-scale-slo.yaml`. The documentation follows the rule file,
  not the comment.
- **The skills count.** One commit message says 14 skills; the tree contains 13
  directories under `src/backend/ai/skills/`. The documentation says 13.
- **The Iris skills, chaining, memory and investigation-surface source files are
  untracked** in the working tree. They are documented because the owner
  instruction was to document the diff up to today, but they are not yet
  committed.
- **`ENABLE_GNMI_CORRELATION` is not live-attested.** The lane exists; it has
  not been proven end to end under mutual TLS. Documented as a known limitation
  rather than as a capability.
- **OSPF depth is graded `doc_claimed`.** No OSPF-speaking SNMP device exists in
  the validation lab, so the OSPF-MIB reads are vendor-documented rather than
  wire-confirmed. IS-IS depth over gNMI is `lab_validated`.
- **Screenshots.** This pass replaced fabricated text output with real captures
  from the running lab stack. It did not capture screenshots; the portal still
  has none, and the vendors we benchmarked use them heavily with descriptive alt
  text. That is the largest remaining gap.
